OpenCommons Health PIM

Data and Information Disclosure Statement

Effective date: July 28, 2026

Plain-language summary. OpenCommons Health PIM is designed to keep identifiable personal health information under the authenticated individual’s control. The Application stores selected records in the owner’s local Solid Pod or owner-controlled application environment. It does not sell personal health information, does not intentionally disclose identifiable health information to non-owner recipients, and does not collect Epic/MyChart usernames or passwords.

1. Purpose of this disclosure

This Data and Information Disclosure Statement explains what categories of information OpenCommons Health PIM may process, where that information is stored, when information may move between systems, what information is not disclosed by design, and what choices the authenticated owner has before information is imported, stored, exported, or released. This document is intended for sandbox registration, local deployment, container deployment, and future iPhone/mobile review workflows.

This statement is not a substitute for legal advice, a HIPAA Notice of Privacy Practices, or a production privacy policy. Legal status and regulatory obligations may vary depending on who deploys the Application, whether a covered entity or business associate is involved, the user’s jurisdiction, and the operational environment. Production or customer activation should be reviewed by qualified legal and privacy counsel.

2. Information the Application may process

With owner configuration and authorization, the Application may process personal health information and related metadata, including: profile and demographic information, conditions, medications, allergies, immunizations, vital signs, providers, lab results, insurance policies, clinical document metadata, workflow task metadata, import provenance, terminology codes, Solid Pod connection status, Epic SMART/FHIR authorization status, owner-visible audit events, and local deployment diagnostics.

The Application may also process non-health technical information needed to operate locally, such as localhost URLs, configured port numbers, Solid Pod base URLs, Epic FHIR endpoint hosts, registered redirect URI hosts and paths, feature flags, and diagnostic status labels. Diagnostic outputs are designed to avoid secrets, tokens, authorization codes, raw FHIR payloads, patient identifiers, and raw personal health information.

3. Information source categories

Information may come from the authenticated owner through manual entry, from the owner’s local Solid Pod, from deterministic sandbox or mock data used during development, from owner-approved Epic SMART on FHIR read access, from future owner-approved HealthKit/iPhone local resources, or from local administrative configuration supplied by the owner/operator.

4. Epic/MyChart and SMART on FHIR authorization

When Epic integration is enabled, the Application uses SMART on FHIR authorization to request access to selected patient-facing FHIR resources. The owner authenticates with Epic/MyChart or the applicable health-system authorization page, not inside OpenCommons Health PIM. The Application must not ask for, log, store, or transmit Epic/MyChart usernames or passwords.

The initial Epic workflow is read-only and owner-mediated. The owner can review requested scopes, run local and live readiness diagnostics, connect through the SMART flow, preview available records, review reconciliation status, and choose whether selected records should be written to the owner’s Solid Pod. Outbound Epic writeback, provider messaging, and task updates are disabled unless a future deployment receives separate health-system approval, scope approval, audit design, and validation.

5. Where information is stored

Identifiable personal health information is intended to remain in the owner-controlled Solid Pod or owner-controlled local application environment. Epic authorization grant material, if stored, is designed to be encrypted before being written to owner-controlled storage. Localhost and container deployments may store data on the owner’s device or local network. Future mobile deployments may use owner-controlled device storage and platform security controls.

6. Information disclosed by design

The Application is not designed to disclose identifiable personal health information to non-owner recipients. Information may move between the owner-authorized external data source, the Application, and the owner’s Solid Pod when the owner initiates or approves a workflow. Anonymized or de-identified release workflows require owner approval and are intended to remove direct identifiers before data is returned through release APIs.

Safe operational diagnostics may disclose non-secret configuration status, such as whether a feature is enabled, whether a URL host is configured, whether a client id is present, whether a redirect path is configured, whether scope names are requested, and whether capability checks succeeded. These diagnostics are intended for troubleshooting and registration readiness without exposing personal health information or credentials.

7. Information not disclosed by design

The Application is designed not to disclose or intentionally expose: Epic/MyChart passwords, Solid passwords, client secrets, encryption keys, access tokens, refresh tokens, authorization codes, raw OAuth grant material, raw personal health records, raw FHIR payloads, patient identifiers, raw clinical documents, source document URLs, device secrets, or personal health information through anonymized release APIs or diagnostics.

8. Owner choices and controls

The owner controls whether to enable Epic integration, whether to use mock, sandbox, or production configuration, whether to start SMART authorization, whether to preview imports, which sections to apply, whether to disconnect, and whether to approve anonymized release. The owner is responsible for protecting local device access, Solid Pod credentials, local environment files, browser sessions, screenshots, logs, backups, and any copied exports.

9. Anonymized release and research-style outputs

The Application includes owner-approved anonymized release workflows for selected local records. These workflows are intended to transform identifiable records before they are released. They should not be used as a guarantee that every dataset is impossible to re-identify, especially when small populations, rare conditions, detailed dates, linked documents, or external datasets could enable re-identification. Use of anonymized outputs must be limited to the owner-approved purpose and should be reviewed before any production or external sharing workflow.

10. Logs, diagnostics, screenshots, and support requests

Support requests, issue reports, screenshots, terminal output, browser logs, and diagnostic exports should not include personal health information, passwords, tokens, authorization codes, client secrets, encryption keys, raw FHIR responses, patient identifiers, or raw document URLs. When evidence is needed, use the safe diagnostics export and review all content before sharing.

11. Third-party systems

Epic, MyChart, health-system FHIR endpoints, Solid Community Server, Apple/iOS services, terminology providers, browsers, operating systems, container runtimes, and network infrastructure are governed by their own terms, policies, and security controls. OpenCommons Health PIM does not control those third-party systems and cannot guarantee their availability, accuracy, authorization decisions, or disclosure practices.

12. Breach, incident, and production review

If the Application is operated beyond personal local development, the operator should establish incident-response, breach-assessment, notification, logging, retention, deletion, support, and audit procedures appropriate to the deployment and applicable law. Health applications and personal health record products may be subject to privacy, security, consumer-protection, breach-notification, health data, platform, or app-store obligations depending on how they are deployed and by whom.

13. Changes to this disclosure

This disclosure may be updated as OpenCommons Health PIM evolves. The effective date above identifies the version distributed with this deployment. Material changes to data collection, storage, use, disclosure, release, or third-party integrations should be reflected in an updated disclosure before those changes are used in a production or customer-facing environment.
